\abstract{Continuous auscultation needs streaming detectors that observe all audio without recomputing expensive internal representations at every step. Existing selective-update methods skip work based on input or representation change or on learned gates, and none estimates which skipped updates matter. We train a lightweight controller on a counterfactual skip-harm utility, the change in the model's output when a region's state update is withheld, and a budget-exact greedy rule updates only the regions of a persistent 64-region frequency graph that are most harmful to skip. We measure how well selective inference preserves the full model's detections; accuracy against the annotations is secondary, because an annotation shift keeps absolute scores low. On the SPRSound paediatric benchmark (three seeds), with 25\% of the graph updates the controller reaches an event-preservation F-score of 0.78, against 0.59 for a gate trained end to end on the task loss, 0.56 for a change-prediction objective and 0.41 for random selection, while detection against the annotations stays non-inferior to full updating. Gains over change-based and random selection hold for a graph gated recurrent unit (GRU) cell, a harmonic graph and the adult HF\_Lung dataset. The end-to-end speedup of our small model is modest (1.14-fold).
}
