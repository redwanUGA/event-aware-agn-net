\abstract{Continuous auscultation needs streaming detectors that observe all audio without recomputing expensive internal representations at every step, but existing selective-update methods skip work based on input or representation change or on learned gates, and none of them estimates which skipped updates matter. We propose Event-Aware AGN-Net, a streaming respiratory sound event detector that keeps a persistent graph of 64 frequency regions and updates the region states only on a budget-limited set chosen by a lightweight controller. The controller is trained on a counterfactual skip-harm utility: the change in the model's output when a region's update is withheld. On the SPRSound paediatric benchmark (three seeds, patient-level bootstrap), with 25\% of the graph updates the controller preserves the full-update model's detections with an event-preservation F-score of 0.78, compared with 0.56 for a change-prediction objective and 0.41 for random selection. The gain holds in every seed and on a later data release, and the event F-score against the annotations is non-inferior to full updating. The advantage carries over to a graph gated recurrent unit (GRU) cell, while the end-to-end speedup is modest (1.14-fold). Predicting the task consequence of skipping, rather than representation change, lets streaming biomedical models decide which updates to compute.
